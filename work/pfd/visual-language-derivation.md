# Perception-first derivation for the book imagery

## Design problem

The reader needs a brief, coherent visual pause at major transitions in a dense book about struggle, meaning, and intelligent machines. The images must add emotional texture without becoming diagrams, chapter summaries, or generic AI decoration.

## L0: Cognitive load

The reader has limited working memory, and the manuscript already asks them to hold complex arguments. A detailed scene or cluster of symbols would compete with the text.

**R1:** Each image must have one dominant form, ample white space, and no text or symbolic inventory. It may suggest one tension, but it must not encode the chapter's full argument.

**Citations:** Cowan (2001) for working-memory capacity; Hassin et al. (2009) for unconscious processing load.

## L1: First impression

The images will appear at high-salience transitions. Generic AI imagery or overly smooth generated surfaces would weaken the perceived care of the edition before the reader studies the details.

**R2:** The images must show convincing physical texture, purposeful irregularity, and a restrained composition at first glance. They must avoid familiar AI-image cues such as neon circuitry, synthetic gloss, and gratuitous detail.

**Citations:** Lindgaard et al. (2006) for rapid visual judgments; Leder et al. (2004) for stages of aesthetic processing.

## L2: Processing fluency

The reader must recognize the images as one family across PDF, EPUB, and the reading site. Large shifts in medium, palette, or composition would make each image feel like a new visual system.

**R3:** Every image must reuse the same background, restrained palette, material vocabulary, gold behavior, spacing, and level-specific aspect ratio. Variation comes from the form and action, not from a new style.

**Citations:** Alter and Oppenheimer (2009) for processing fluency; Spence (2011) for cross-modal coherence.

## L3: Perception and expectation

Readers have seen robots, glowing brains, and circuit-board portraits used to signal AI. Those symbols predict a technical explainer and narrow the book's human argument. Kintsugi alone can also predict a familiar self-help message about becoming stronger after damage.

**R4:** The visual family must keep the repair and the machine in one unresolved form. Gold must change from irregular fracture repair into precise circuit geometry, so neither the technology nor the healing metaphor fully owns the image.

**Citations:** Clark (2013) for predictive processing; Seckler et al. (2015) for visual coherence as a trust input.

## L4: Reading behavior

The image should cause a short pause, then return the reader to the text. If every section uses a large plate, the pause becomes routine and the images lose their function.

**R5:** Use large plates only for the three parts. Use smaller vignettes at selected chapter openings after the part system is proven. The final placement must preserve the reading sequence and include concise alt text.

**Citations:** Hertwig and Erev (2009) for experience-based understanding; Trope and Liberman (2010) for matching expression to psychological distance.

## Accumulated requirements

- R1: One dominant form, white space, no text, and no summary-by-symbols.
- R2: Convincing physical texture, purposeful irregularity, and no generic generated-image cues.
- R3: One stable family across formats, with variation limited to form and action.
- R4: Gold changes from fracture repair into circuitry within the same unresolved form.
- R5: Three large part plates first; selected chapter vignettes only after the system holds together.

## Derived solution

Use isolated mixed-media forms made from fibrous paper, mineral pigment, charcoal, clay, or stone. Repair visible breaks with imperfect warm gold leaf, then let those seams tighten into sparse circuit paths. Keep the canvas white and the composition asymmetric. Use one large plate per part and reserve smaller images for chapter openings where a distinct metaphor earns the pause.

Three studies should hold the form and composition constant while changing the medium. This separates the choice of visual language from the choice of chapter metaphor.

## Existing proposal check

- **Kintsugi:** satisfies the need for breakage, repair, and material texture. It fails R4 when used alone because it reads as a complete healing metaphor.
- **Gold circuit traces:** satisfies the machine connection and existing book palette. It fails R2 and R4 when used as surface decoration or familiar technology ornament.
- **Watercolor:** satisfies softness and visible texture. It can fail R2 when it becomes a generic storybook wash or loses detail in print.
- **White, borderless images:** satisfies R1 and supports the existing page design. Generated edges still need a production check to confirm that the white reaches every canvas edge.

## Gap

No single proposed element defines how broken matter becomes machine structure. The production rule must specify that transition: a real fracture begins as an irregular gold repair and gradually resolves into circuit geometry.
