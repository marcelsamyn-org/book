## Passages for the author to reconsider

These are substantive questions, not copyedits. The worktree leaves the prose intact. Search by the quoted words; line numbers will move.

- **“five independent variables”**: Seligman's elements can be defined and measured separately, but their scores correlate. Does “five elements of well-being” express the intended point?
- **“Psychology distinguishes three time spans”**: OECD describes components, not time spans; life satisfaction is one component, not the umbrella. Is the contrast about momentary affect, reflective evaluation, and meaningful functioning?
- **“Hedonia is the ‘right now’”**: the contrast is not simply short versus long term. Hedonic well-being also includes life satisfaction. The active draft's “They're the same thing” would make the distinction less accurate.
- **“People who reported high meaning often reported lower momentary positive affect when engaged in effortful tasks”**: the cited Baumeister study did not measure that task-level effect. Its broad findings concern surveys and partial correlations. Remove this bullet or identify a separate task-level source; the first three bullets broadly match [the paper](https://doi.org/10.1080/17439760.2013.830764).
- **“return to their baseline level of happiness after just a few months”**: no matching lottery evidence was found. The long-run lottery study supports sustained gains in life satisfaction. What narrower adaptation claim is needed here?
- **“they judge it much more from a long-term perspective”**: the forecasting review supports prediction errors, but does not establish this general rule for purchases. Which contrast matters to the argument: predicted versus experienced feelings, or pleasure versus life evaluation?
- **“Just pleasure without meaning leads to boredom, apathy, addiction, aggression”**: the youth study supports the final association with suicidal ideation, not this causal chain. “Existential vacuum” is a theoretical concept, not a demonstrated sequence of these outcomes.
- **“They can only do that if” / “if it doesn’t all connect together”**: coherence relates to well-being; neither source establishes that a narrative is required to judge life satisfaction, or that pleasure produces disconnected lives.
- **“benefits that we don’t get from chasing pleasure alone” / “we break down easily”**: the benefit studies do not establish that these outcomes are exclusive to eudaimonia. Huta and Ryan found overlapping benefits and advantages when both motives were present. Seery does not test a pleasure-only mechanism or the proposed causal mechanism through “proof” of strength. No new citation was added to that mechanism.
- **“never learned to care for or take responsibility”**: this is not the research definition of learned helplessness. The relevant issue is uncontrollability and learned control, not irresponsibility or dependence on validation.
- **“if they don’t contribute they can feel lonely and isolated”**: the helping study does not isolate this effect among already-connected people. Its supported condition is autonomous motivation to help.
- **“Because eudaimonia requires short-term moments of stress” / “literally improves your health”**: the studies do not show that stress is required or that it causes health gains. Biomarker associations are observational; the gene-expression interpretation was challenged. Can this paragraph describe associations and their limits instead?
- **“long-term happiness is, in a way, caused by short-term unhappiness” / “re-wiring our minds”**: the sources do not establish this necessity or neural mechanism. The possible value of processing adversity is a narrower claim.
- **“That’s *resilience*, the ability to spring back”**: Bonanno distinguishes maintaining stable functioning from recovery after disruption. Which process does this passage mean?
- **“defined four steps”**: these are components of a proposed processing model, not a universal fixed sequence. Does calling them parts of a model preserve the intended explanation?
- **“If you experienced something truly horrible, it’s just…trauma”**: the curve concerns PTSD symptoms versus perceived growth, not objective event severity. Severe distress and growth can coexist. Lifetime-adversity evidence from Seery concerns a different relationship.
- **“The research isn’t out on that yet”**: Frazier directly studied perceived versus measured growth in 2009. The active draft already removes this sentence; preserve that removal at integration.
- **“leads to a greater sense of life satisfaction” / “gifts that we couldn’t have gained through any other way”**: Seery's study is observational. Roepke's existing positive-event study and Vohs's review do not prove that adverse experiences are necessary or that every deliberate challenge improves satisfaction.
- **“the happiness ‘curve’ even flattens completely”**: keep the 2010 finding distinct from a current general claim. The 2023 paper shows why an across-the-board ceiling is not established.
