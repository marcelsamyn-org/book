# New World, New Scarcity — rewrite checklist, 2026-08-29

*Fifteen items for one chapter. Thirteen are edits; two confirm that a passage stays as written. Each item has **What** (the edit), **Why** (the reason), and **Your call** (the part left to you). Locations are grep-able quotes from `book.mdx`, because line numbers drift.*

*Two words used throughout. A **step** is one link in the sequence where a thing gets cheap and pushes the value onto the next thing: execution, attention, judgement, responsibility. A **section** is a `###` heading inside the chapter.*

*The problem: New World, New Scarcity lists three things AI cannot do. The chapter after it, AI as Infinite Creation, opens by saying that listing things AI cannot do is the wrong method (`"we have to go to the extreme"`). So the two chapters currently contradict each other. Turning the list into the sequence from your TODO note fixes this, because the reader watches each answer fail one at a time instead of being told afterward that they all failed.*

## Cuts — four passages to remove first

- [x] **Cut the opening recap paragraph**
  - *What:* Delete `"Large language models are trained on most of what humans have created"`, or reduce it to a clause.
  - *Why:* This is the fourth time the book explains that AI is best at what has been done a million times. It appears in the Creativity chapter, again later in this chapter, and again in the Conclusion as The Inevitable.
  - *Your call:* If you keep a clause, does it need to say more than "trained on the past, best at the repeated"?
- [x] **Cut the two closing paragraphs**
  - *What:* Delete `"Everything *else* is still the human domain"` and `"Artificial intelligence is the fastest, most dangerously flowing river"`.
  - *Why:* Both images already close the next chapter. Infinite Creation has `"those who shape the riverbanks"` and the line about a song written with the blood of someone heartbroken. Reading each image twice makes the second one land weaker.
  - *Your call:* Nothing is deleted from the book, only from this chapter. Read Infinite Creation's ending afterward and check it still works alone.
- [x] **Cut the creativity recap in Long-term risky activities**
  - *What:* Delete `"You remember from our discussion on creativity"` and the paragraph about novelty being trained out of the model.
  - *Why:* Same fact as the opening recap, one section apart. The founder argument works without it.
  - *Your call:* The founder argument still needs one supporting clause. Either that startups are far outside the training data, or that training pushes the model toward the expected answer. Which one do you want to use here?
- [x] **Cut the advice to become known before the window closes**
  - *What:* Delete `"limited time to differentiate yourself"` and the paragraph containing it.
  - *Why:* It is career advice in a chapter that has stopped giving career advice, and the only place in Part 3 that tells the reader to hurry. The paragraph before it, `"see-saw effect between highly produced and edited content"`, makes the point on its own and stays.
  - *Your call:* If you want to keep the urgency somewhere, the Work chapter is a better fit.

## The sequence — rebuild the middle

- [x] **Decide the chapter is a sequence, not a list**
  - *What:* Commit to the structure in your TODO note. Four things a human supplies to get work done, ordered so that each one getting cheap is what makes the next one scarce: execution, attention, judgement, responsibility. AI takes the first three. It cannot take the fourth.
  - *Why:* This is the only chapter that can work through the obvious answers and show each one failing. Once it does, Infinite Creation's opening reads as the next step rather than as a correction. It also matches the chapter title.
  - *Your call:* A list lets the reader keep the three answers. The sequence takes them away and gives nothing back until the next chapter, so the reader spends about a page with no answer at all. That sets up the metaphysics better. Is that a page you want them to sit through?
- [x] **Keep execution as the first step, and add its earlier round**
  - *What:* `"When AI can make pretty much everything you ask it to"` through `"the value of a funny video of a cat playing an instrument"` stays as written. Add a short paragraph before it saying execution already got cheap once: publishing, distribution and recommendation, which is the social media era covered in `"already happen with social media"`. Attention became the bottleneck as a result.
  - *Why:* It starts the sequence on something the reader has lived through rather than on a claim about AI, and it explains where attention scarcity came from, so the second step has somewhere to start from.
  - *Your call:* Name the attention economy, or keep it as your own experience of scrolling?
- [x] **Add attention as the second step**
  - *What:* Agents now read, filter and monitor for you, so the attention that cheap publishing made scarce becomes cheap in its turn.
  - *Why:* Without it the sequence jumps from making things to judging them, and skips the part the reader spends most of their working day on.
  - *Your call:* Two different attentions are in play here — the one advertisers buy, and the one you spend at work deciding what is worth looking at. Social media made the first scarce, agents are taking the second. Separate them, or let the reader feel the overlap?
- [x] **Add judgement as the third step**
  - *What:* Where the chapter says value moves to describing and judging, add that judgement gets cheap too. You have already written this twice elsewhere: `"even at that step of the ladder, AI might just be able to step right along with us"` in Decision-Making, and `"need to rely on it even for judgement"` in Meaningful Work.
  - *Why:* Without this step, the chapter leaves taste standing as an answer, and the book has already argued that taste does not hold.
  - *Your call:* You think taste lasts longer than execution but not forever. Do you want to say how much longer, or leave it unquantified?
- [x] **Keep the lamp and the inner-world paragraph together**
  - *What:* No rewrite. `"obsessed with lights and light quality"` and `"I see this as an opportunity for us humans to strengthen our inner world"` stay in that order, positioned after the judgement step and before Responsibility.
  - *Why:* The lamp is the last worked-example from your own life before the chapter turns to what humans are for, and it echoes the Conclusion's closing scene at the sideboard. The inner-world paragraph is where the chapter switches subject, from what AI takes to what you build in yourself.
  - *Your call:* The inner-world paragraph currently passes in a few lines. Expand it, or leave it at that length?
- [x] **Merge Long-term risky activities into Responsibility**
  - *What:* Fold `"ChatGPT, make me a billion dollar company"` and the founder argument into the Responsibility section. Delete the separate heading.
  - *Why:* Both describe carrying consequence, at two lengths of time: the notary at the moment of signing, the founder over several years. Merging them also removes the third telling of how the model is trained.
  - *Your call:* Which one opens the merged section? The notary is concrete and unexpected. The founder is the one readers already have opinions about.
- [x] **End the sequence on Responsibility and say why it stops there**
  - *What:* Put Responsibility last. Near `"An artificial intelligence is not a legal entity"`, add a sentence saying this step is different: the earlier three fell because AI got better at them, and getting better at a task does not make something able to be sued.
  - *Why:* The reader currently has to work out for themselves why the sequence stops. Saying it also means the argument does not rest on a claim about what AI can do, which could stop being true.
  - *Your call:* `"The natural breaks they had because writing is an inherently slower process"` says the notary's job gets worse, not better. Keep that, or end the section on the value rather than the cost?
- [x] **Put Personality after the sequence and say it was never part of it**
  - *What:* Move the Personality section (`"One day I had an idea to make a chatbot prompt / personality, Dave"`) to after Responsibility. Open it by saying the question has changed, from what work is left to when you would still choose a person.
  - *Why:* Personality does not behave like the other four. AI personalities are already cheap: Dave exists, character.ai exists. Nothing about it becomes obsolete and pushes value onto a next thing, so it is not a step. Ending the chapter here also leads into most of Infinite Creation, which is about presence and stillness (`"Those who strip away everything they do and just *are*"`).
  - *Your call:* This ends the chapter on preference rather than on consequence. Responsibility is the harder claim, and the Conclusion relies on it (`"The notary making the call, the artist writing the love song"`). If you want the chapter to end on that instead, swap the two and accept that Personality interrupts the sequence.
- [x] **End on the question instead of a summary**
  - *What:* Close by asking what happens when creation itself gets cheap, rather than restating what survived.
  - *Why:* Infinite Creation answers that question. Its last line, `"What remains? Consecration."`, names both things this chapter leaves standing: setting something apart, and binding it to responsibility.
  - *Your call:* Designing Aligned Allies already ends on a question (`"So what happens when artificial intelligence keeps on becoming more and more intelligent?"`). Two in a row makes it a pattern. Do you want that, or a different ending here?

## Outside this chapter — two problems in nearby chapters

- [ ] **Shorten the alignment sections in Designing Aligned Allies**
  - *What:* Cut down Alignment Research and Teaching AI Good Behavior. `"We discussed earlier how"` re-explains the screenplay idea from Part 1's `"Language Models are Screenplay Writers"`, and `"Synthetic Document Fine-Tuning"` explains how training works rather than making an argument.
  - *Why:* It is the longest stretch of how-the-technology-works explanation in Part 3, and it sits directly before this chapter.
  - *Your call:* How much of the alignment detail does your argument need, and how much is there because it is interesting? Only you know which examples you would miss.
- [ ] **Keep "you" the same person across four chapters**
  - *What:* Check who the reader is in Mental Gym, Designing Aligned Allies, this chapter, and Infinite Creation. Right now it is the individual, then the AI labs, then the worker, then the soul.
  - *Why:* The two-part rise you described works because one person moves through it. Four different readers in a row breaks that, and it is the most likely reason the practical half and the metaphysical half of Part 3 do not feel joined.
  - *Your call:* The simplest fix is writing Designing Aligned Allies as what you should demand and choose, instead of as a report on what the labs do. That changes the voice of a whole chapter, so decide it before rewriting this one.
